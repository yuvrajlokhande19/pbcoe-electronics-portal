1. Introduction & Project Background

In contemporary higher education institutions, manual pen-and-paper attendance logging remains prone to severe operational inefficiencies, including fraudulent proxy roll calls, physical ledger loss, transcription errors during sessional auditing, and substantial faculty instructional time loss during laboratory periods. Under the National Education Policy (NEP 2020) experiential learning framework, this laboratory activity focuses on the complete practical design, circuit interfacing, firmware programming, and deployment of a cost-optimized, standalone IoT-Enabled Biometric Attendance Telemetry System.

The engineered embedded device incorporates an ESP32 dual-core Xtensa 32-bit LX6 microcontroller directly interfaced to an industrial R307 optical fingerprint sensor and an active piezo acoustic confirmation chime. The node connects autonomously to campus Wi-Fi infrastructure to transmit cryptographic HTTPS REST payloads to a serverless Supabase PostgreSQL cloud database, synchronizing student attendance records in less than 350 milliseconds with the department web portal (etcianspbcoe.dpdns.org) without requiring an attached host computer or commercial software licenses.

2. Theoretical Principles of Biometric Sensing & Optical FTIR Physics

Biometric identification is grounded in the physiological permanence and uniqueness of human friction ridges on the epidermis. While global ridge geometries follow archetypal classes (loops, whorls, and arches), individual biometric differentiation is established through localized ridge anomalies termed Minutiae Points — predominantly ridge endings (abrupt terminations of friction ridges) and ridge bifurcations (points where a single friction ridge diverges into two distinct branches).

Optical Sensing Physics (Frustrated Total Internal Reflection - FTIR):
The R307 sensor employs an optical glass prism illuminated by internal blue/red LEDs at an incident angle exceeding the critical angle (θc). When dry air contacts the polished glass hypotenuse, Total Internal Reflection (TIR) occurs, projecting 100% of incident light onto the internal 256×288 pixel CMOS photodiode array, producing a bright uniform image. When a student places a finger against the prism surface, elevated epidermal ridges make direct optical contact with the glass, eliminating the refractive index mismatch and frustrating total reflection. Light escapes through the skin ridges, resulting in high-contrast dark pixels, whereas recessed valleys maintain an air gap, producing bright reflections.

The integrated digital signal processor (DSP) inside the R307 digitizes this optical raw image at 500 DPI spatial resolution. Ridge filtering and binarization algorithms isolate minutiae coordinate vectors $(x, y, \theta)$, condensing them into an irreversible 512-byte mathematical characterization template. During live authentication, this template is matched against stored templates across flash slots #1 to #65 via high-speed 1:N correlation.

Mathematical Formulation of FTIR Critical Angle:
The critical angle θc is governed by Snell's Law: θc = arcsin(nmedium / nglass). For glass (n ≈ 1.52) and air (n ≈ 1.00), θc ≈ 41.1°. When epidermal ridges (n ≈ 1.44) make contact, θc increases to ≈ 71.3°, completely frustrating total internal reflection and producing sharp, high-contrast dark ridges on the optical sensor plane.

UART Protocol Frame Structure:
Serial communication operates at 57,600 baud (8 data bits, 1 stop bit, no parity). Packets follow strict framing: Header (0xEF01) → Module Address (0xFFFFFFFF) → Package PID (0x01 Command / 0x07 Data / 0x02 Ack) → Packet Length → Payload Instruction → 16-bit Checksum.

7. End-to-End System Architecture & Telemetry Pipeline

The biometric telemetry platform establishes a robust multi-layer IoT architecture spanning the physical edge, local serial communications, cloud REST gateways, and the responsive web portal:

Fig 4.1: Technical Engineering Architecture — Edge Sensing Node, ESP32 Cloud Gateway & Supabase PostgreSQL Stream
1. Edge Verification Engine:
The R307 optical prism captures fingertip minutiae and compares vectors against flash memory templates #1 to #65. Upon positive match, candidate slot ID and confidence score are transferred over UART2 in <280ms.
2. Supabase Cloud Pipeline:
The ESP32 constructs a structured JSON payload and dispatches an HTTPS POST over TLS 1.3 to /rest/v1/attendance_logs, triggering instantaneous database replication across active portal sessions.
8. Dual-Pass Optical Enrollment & Verification State Machine

To eliminate erroneous partial scans and minimize false rejections, candidate registration strictly enforces a 4-state dual-pass protocol executed cooperatively between the web portal and the embedded firmware:

Four-Stage Optical Enrollment Protocol:
• State 1 (Primary Optical Acquisition): Sensor LED illuminates. Candidate places fingertip on prism. First image is captured and converted into character buffer 1 (CharBuffer1). Buzzer sounds an 80ms pulse.
• State 2 (Finger Removal Detection): Firmware loop polls finger.getImage() until FINGERPRINT_NOFINGER is asserted, preventing accidental duplicate sampling of the same touch.
• State 3 (Secondary Validation Pass): Candidate touches sensor again. Second image is digitized and converted into CharBuffer2.
• State 4 (Vector Correlation & Flash Commitment): Firmware triggers finger.createModel(). If correlation coefficient exceeds 92%, combined template is committed to assigned slot ID (1 to 65). Double acoustic chime confirms success.
Dual-Core FreeRTOS Task Scheduling & Fault Recovery:
To ensure zero scan lag during network latency, the firmware executes cooperative multitasking on the ESP32: Core 1 executes the real-time biometric scanning task with deterministic sub-millisecond priority, while Core 0 manages the TCP/IP stack, TLS cryptographic handshakes, and HTTPS telemetry transmission. If Wi-Fi disconnects, check-in records are buffered locally in non-volatile flash (NVS) and automatically flushed upon reconnection.
Cryptographic Payload Integrity & REST Schema:
Each attendance record dispatched to Supabase conforms to strict schema validation: {"student_prn": 24121227, "slot_id": 16, "status": "PRESENT", "auth_mode": "BIOMETRIC_FTIR", "device_mac": "ESP32_ETC_01", "timestamp_utc": "2026-10-26T04:30:00Z"}. Row-Level Security (RLS) policies restrict inserts solely to authenticated node tokens.
